# PRD - Merchant Fee Negotiation Table

## One-liner
Korean restaurant owners compare delivery-app fee pressure and prepare a collective bargaining packet without drowning in platform terms.

## Problem
Korean mom-and-pop restaurants, franchisees, and local merchant groups facing delivery app fee negotiations. are reacting to fresh market/community signals but still solve the work with scattered screenshots, invoices, threads, or ad hoc spreadsheets. The prototype should turn that repeated pain into one focused first-screen workflow.

## Target user
Korean mom-and-pop restaurants, franchisees, and local merchant groups facing delivery app fee negotiations.

## Key UX
A collective fee table turns receipts, commission tiers, promo claims, and dispute examples into a negotiation packet for merchant associations.

## Required UI sections
- Fee burden comparison by platform
- Promo/free-delivery cost pass-through tracker
- Peer benchmark cards by cuisine/order size
- Dispute evidence checklist
- Negotiation packet preview in Korean

## Design profile
- financial-analyst

## Theme
- Korea small merchant fee leverage

- benchmarking workbench + negotiation memo

## Twist
- Localizes the cost model around Korean delivery-app collective bargaining rather than generic restaurant analytics.

## Small group fit
- Merchant associations need shared evidence now because policy and fee negotiations are live.

## Spread trigger
- A merchant can show a printable packet to nearby owners and recruit them into the same evidence pool.

## Copy/paste Stitch prompt
Create an analytical workbench called “Merchant Fee Negotiation Table” for Korean restaurant owners comparing delivery app fee pressure. Include platform fee rows, order-size scenarios, free-delivery promo burden, peer benchmark cards, dispute examples, and a Korean negotiation packet preview. Design direction: use the financial-analyst profile with institutional ink/off-white styling, restrained green/warning cues, high-signal tables, and written interpretation beside each chart.

## Non-goals
- Do not implement a custom local demo in Phase A.
- Do not present source signals as proof of market size.
- Do not turn the product into a generic dashboard without the specific trigger and workflow.

## Success metrics
- A viewer understands the target user and core action within 5 seconds.
- The primary workflow can be inferred from the first screen without explanatory marketing copy.
- The Stitch output preserves the assigned design profile and includes all required sections.
