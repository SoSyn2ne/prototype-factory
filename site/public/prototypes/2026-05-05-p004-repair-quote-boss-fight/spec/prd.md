# PRD — Repair Quote Boss Fight

## Problem
- A playful repair-or-replace arena that turns broken gadgets, warranty rules, parts delays, and quote anxiety into a shareable savings battle.
- Inspired by external problem signals: Signal 7 — Taylor Wessing — EU repair obligations arriving in 2026: https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2026/01/pflicht-zur-reparatur-2026; Signal 8 — Intelligent Living — Right to Repair goes live July 31, 2026: https://www.intelligentliving.co/eu-right-to-repair-quotes-parts-fixing/

## Target user
- Consumers with cracked phones, tired appliances, broken headphones, or battery problems who suspect replacement is being pushed too quickly.

## Key UX
- Describe the broken item and quote; the app stages a boss fight between repair, replace, warranty extension, parts delay, and resale value, then exports a funny “I saved it” receipt.

## Required UI sections
- broken gadget intake
- repair-vs-replace battle arena
- warranty extension power-up
- parts availability quest
- quote fairness meter
- landfill shame mini-boss
- shareable saved-it receipt

## Design profile
- playful-experimental

## Stitch prompt
Design a playful consumer web prototype called Repair Quote Boss Fight for people deciding whether to repair or replace a broken phone, appliance, headphones, or battery-powered gadget. Users should enter the item, symptoms, repair quote, replacement price, warranty status, parts delay, and emotional attachment, then watch repair, replace, warranty extension, resale value, and landfill shame battle as game characters. Include a broken gadget intake, repair-vs-replace battle arena, warranty extension power-up, parts availability quest, quote fairness meter, landfill shame mini-boss, and shareable saved-it receipt. Design direction: use a playful-experimental aesthetic with arcade battle cards, expressive repair monsters, bright victory states, comic microcopy, and readable cost math so the prototype feels funny, practical, and instantly shareable.
