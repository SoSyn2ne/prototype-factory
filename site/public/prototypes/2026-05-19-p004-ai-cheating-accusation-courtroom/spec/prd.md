# PRD — AI Cheating Accusation Courtroom

## One-liner
A playful family-school debate board that helps students turn scary AI-detector accusations into a process timeline, evidence scrapbook, and appeal script.

## Candidate type
Fun / Experimental

## Problem
- A playful family-school debate board that helps students turn scary AI-detector accusations into a process timeline, evidence scrapbook, and appeal script.
- Inspired by external problem signals Signal 7 and Signal 8 from today's search sweep.

## Target user
- Students, parents, tutors, school counselors, and writing coaches facing AI-detector accusations, especially when a teacher relies on detector screenshots, awkward writing style, or a sudden prove-you-wrote-it demand.

## Key UX
Paste the accusation, detector screenshot summary, essay draft, version history, outline notes, teacher comments, and student memory fragments; the product builds a courtroom-style evidence wall, flags weak detector claims, rehearses a calm appeal conversation, and creates a shareable human writing receipt.

## Required UI sections
- accusation intake stage
- detector screenshot skepticism meter
- draft/version-history scrapbook
- student memory evidence cards
- teacher meeting rehearsal script
- appeal timeline and escalation ladder
- shareable human writing receipt

## Stitch prompt
Design a playful experimental education product called AI Cheating Accusation Courtroom for students, parents, tutors, school counselors, and writing coaches facing AI-detector accusations. Users paste the accusation, detector screenshot summary, essay draft, version history, outline notes, teacher comments, and student memory fragments; the app builds a courtroom-style evidence wall, flags weak detector claims, rehearses a calm appeal conversation, and creates a shareable human writing receipt. Include an accusation intake stage, detector screenshot skepticism meter, draft/version-history scrapbook, student memory evidence cards, teacher meeting rehearsal script, appeal timeline and escalation ladder, and shareable human writing receipt. Design direction: use a playful-experimental aesthetic with school-notebook textures, courtroom exhibit cards, bold red objection stamps, friendly but sharp microcopy, and a clear single path from panic to prepared appeal so it feels portfolio-worthy without mocking the student.
