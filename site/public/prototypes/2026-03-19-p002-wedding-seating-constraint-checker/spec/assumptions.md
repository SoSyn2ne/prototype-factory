# Assumptions (KNOWN / ASSUMPTION / UNKNOWN)

## KNOWN
- Most seating conflicts can be expressed as simple statements: "A and B should not sit together" or "this group should sit together".
- Couples often iterate quickly: draft → see conflict → swap → repeat.

## ASSUMPTION
- A conflict checker is valuable even without an optimization engine (it reduces embarrassing mistakes).
- Users will accept a naive initial draft as long as conflicts are clearly shown.
- The minimal UI can be “list-first” (not a draggable floor plan) for speed.

## UNKNOWN
- How many constraint types are needed to be useful (avoid/together vs. plus/minus preferences, “seat near” etc.).
- The right abstraction for real weddings: tables, rounds, head table, kids table, plus-ones.
- Whether users strongly prefer a drag-and-drop canvas (could increase complexity significantly).
