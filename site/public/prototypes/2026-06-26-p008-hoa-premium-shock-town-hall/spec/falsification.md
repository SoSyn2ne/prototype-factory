# Falsification checklist (5-7)

1. Does the first screen solve the trigger moment instead of becoming a generic dashboard?
2. Are the cited source signals visible in sample data and workflow language?
3. Does the assigned design profile (field-ops) clearly shape layout, density, and controls?
4. Is there a concrete output artifact the user would share or act on?
5. Is the theme (자산 방어) visible without theme-washing the idea?
6. Would the target user recognize their current workaround in the product?
7. Does the Stitch prompt avoid custom implementation instructions outside Phase A?

## Stitch prompt under test
Build a production-grade web app prototype called "HOA Premium Shock Town Hall" for condo boards and property managers explaining insurance non-renewal or premium hikes to anxious owners. The first screen must be a working meeting-room projector tool with town-hall mode, premium shock explainer, carrier quote timeline, dues/reserve impact slider, roof/claim evidence wall, owner question queue, and vote-ready tradeoff summary. Use realistic sample data: carrier non-renewal letter, 4x quote, roof age concern, wildfire/flood risk note, reserve balance, $150/month dues impact, and three owner questions. Design direction: use the field-ops profile with large readable meeting controls, high-contrast status rows, reliable steel and graphite tones, and sparing orange/cyan accents for decision points. Do not make a marketing landing page; the first screen must be the working app.
