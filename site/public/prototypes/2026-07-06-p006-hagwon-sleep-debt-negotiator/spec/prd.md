# PRD — Hagwon Sleep Debt Negotiator

## Problem
- A parent-facing tradeoff tool that compares private education spending against sleep, stress, and status pressure.
- Source evidence: S12 - Reddit r/Living_in_Korea discussion on hagwon costs: Korean private education spending remains a visible status and budget pressure point for parents.

## Target user
- Korean parents balancing hagwon costs, peer pressure, child fatigue, and fear of falling behind.

## Key UX
- Enter classes, monthly costs, commute, bedtime, and goals; the product creates a family negotiation sheet with tradeoff scenarios.

## Required UI sections
- Class cost stack
- Sleep debt meter
- Peer pressure notes
- Tradeoff scenarios
- Family agreement sheet
- Share card

## Design profile
- calm-consumer
- Design direction must be tailored inside the Stitch prompt and avoid generic dashboard styling.

## Theme
- 사교육 / 외로움

## Source signal references
- S12: Reddit r/Living_in_Korea discussion on hagwon costs — https://www.reddit.com/r/Living_in_Korea/comments/1qsumqa/these_are_the_private_academies_%ED%95%99%EC%9B%90_hagwon_that/

## Stitch prompt
Create a responsive web prototype for "Hagwon Sleep Debt Negotiator". Target user: Korean parents who feel pressure to add private academies while their child loses sleep and the monthly budget keeps rising. Core UX: the user enters classes, monthly cost, commute time, bedtime, academic goal, and peer-pressure notes; the product compares status anxiety against sleep debt and outputs three family negotiation scenarios. Required sections: class cost stack, sleep debt meter, peer-pressure notes, scenario comparison, family agreement sheet, and shareable summary card. Use the calm-consumer design profile: emotionally steady guidance, warm neutral surfaces, gentle accent, clear tradeoff cards, and helper text that avoids blaming parents or children.

## Success metrics
- A Stitch operator can paste the prompt without rewriting it.
- The first screen shows the actual workflow, not a landing page.
- The output artifact is visible before the fold or immediately after the primary input.
