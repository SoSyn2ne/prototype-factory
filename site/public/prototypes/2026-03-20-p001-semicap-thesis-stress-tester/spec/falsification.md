# Falsification checklist (5–7)

1. **The score is unhelpful:** Users can’t explain why the risk score changed after moving a slider.
2. **Wrong assumption mapping:** In 5 common shock scenarios, users disagree with >50% of “BROKEN” flags.
3. **Too generic:** Users immediately ask for company-specific knobs (customer concentration, product mix) and won’t use a macro-only tool.
4. **Workflow mismatch:** Users want to attach evidence (links/notes) and export, otherwise it doesn’t fit their thesis workflow.
5. **Overreaction bias:** The tool encourages panic (everything becomes BROKEN too easily) rather than structured verification.
6. **Missing key shock types:** Users repeatedly cite shocks not represented (inventory correction, price wars, yield/ramp delays, competitor leapfrogs).
7. **Offline constraint fails:** Users expect live data and abandon an offline-only prototype.
