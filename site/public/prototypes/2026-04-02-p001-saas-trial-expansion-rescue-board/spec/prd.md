# PRD — SaaS Trial Expansion Rescue Board

## One-liner
A revenue-ops workspace that shows which self-serve trial accounts are most likely to expand this week and what friction is blocking conversion.

## Problem
- PLG teams often know which trials activated late only after the conversion window has already cooled.
- Buying signals, product usage gaps, and stakeholder intent are scattered across lifecycle tools and CRM notes.
- Operators need a single rescue screen that ranks near-win accounts and recommends the fastest intervention.

## Target user
- Primary: PLG revenue ops leads, lifecycle marketers, and sales-assist teams at B2B SaaS companies.
- Secondary: product growth managers reviewing expansion forecast risk.

## Why now
- Trial conversion efficiency matters more as paid acquisition stays expensive.
- More B2B SaaS companies are blending self-serve and sales-assist motions.
- Teams have enough telemetry but not enough decision UX.

## Key UX
- Scan the expansion-risk queue and see which accounts deserve human rescue now.
- Open one account and understand activation friction, buying signals, and stakeholder coverage in a single glance.
- Pick the next rescue move and estimate this week’s forecast recovery.

## Required UI sections
- Conversion KPI hero
- Account rescue queue
- Activation-friction timeline
- Stakeholder signal panel
- Recommended next action drawer
- Weekly forecast rail

## Constraints
- Time: Phase A only; no custom demo implementation.
- Budget: should fit lean growth and revenue teams without enterprise setup cost.
- Platform: desktop web dashboard.

## Non-goals
- Writing outbound emails automatically.
- Full CRM synchronization.
- Deep cohort analytics or BI replacement.

## Success metrics
- Increase rescued-trial conversion rate.
- Reduce time-to-action on high-intent stalled accounts.
- Improve forecast confidence for near-term expansion pipeline.

## Stitch-ready design direction
Use a sharp revenue-ops style with midnight navy surfaces, cool slate neutrals, and electric lime accents; keep hierarchy crisp, dense panels highly scannable, and the overall product feel analytical, urgent, and conversion-focused.
