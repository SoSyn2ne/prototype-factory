# PRD — Freight Detention Fee Recovery Desk

## One-liner
A logistics finance workspace that surfaces shipments most likely to recover detention and demurrage fees before reimbursement windows expire.

## Problem
- Carriers, terminals, and drayage partners generate fee disputes that are easy to miss until filing windows close.
- Timeline evidence lives across emails, TMS events, spreadsheets, and PDFs, which slows recovery.
- Teams need one operating screen that ranks reclaimable dollars and shows the fastest next claim action.

## Target user
- Primary: freight audit teams, 3PL operators, and finance managers handling carrier disputes.
- Secondary: operations leaders tracking weekly recovered cash and preventable fee leakage.

## Why now
- Port and yard congestion volatility creates more accessorial disputes.
- Margin pressure makes cash recovery operationally meaningful.
- Teams already have the data but not a clean claims workflow.

## Key UX
- Scan recovery opportunities ranked by dollars at risk and filing urgency.
- Open a shipment and review timeline evidence, carrier events, and supporting documents in one place.
- Take the next claim move with confidence and understand expected weekly cash recovery.

## Required UI sections
- Recovery KPI hero
- Disputed-shipment queue
- Port-and-carrier event timeline
- Evidence bundle panel
- Claim action drawer
- Weekly recovered-cash rail

## Constraints
- Time: Phase A only; no custom demo implementation.
- Budget: must fit lean audit and ops teams without heavy systems work.
- Platform: desktop web workspace.

## Non-goals
- Automatic carrier filing submission.
- Full transportation management system replacement.
- Deep contract-rate authoring.

## Success metrics
- Increase recovered detention and demurrage dollars.
- Reduce average time-to-file on high-confidence disputes.
- Lower preventable expiry of reimbursement opportunities.

## Stitch-ready design direction
Use a disciplined operations-finance style with deep navy surfaces, fog gray neutrals, and bright amber accents; keep hierarchy tight, evidence cards and timelines extremely legible, and the product feel rigorous, cash-conscious, and high-accountability.
