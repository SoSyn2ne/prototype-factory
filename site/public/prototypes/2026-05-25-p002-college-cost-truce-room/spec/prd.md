# PRD - College Cost Truce Room

## One-liner
A family decision room that turns dream-school tension into a calm budget, loan, scholarship, and transfer-path negotiation before applications harden.

## Candidate type
Practical / Commercial

## Target user
Middle-income parents and college-bound students who need a shared plan before admissions, aid letters, and Parent PLUS loans become emotional fights.

## Problem
The target user is facing an emotionally charged, expensive, or reputation-sensitive decision where raw information is scattered across messages, screenshots, bills, device metrics, or family/operator memory. The prototype should turn that scattered situation into one clear guided workflow.

## Key UX
Parent and student enter budget limits, dream schools, in-state options, scholarship targets, transfer paths, and debt boundaries; the app turns them into a negotiated school list and conversation script.

## Required UI sections
- Family budget boundary intake
- Dream-school attachment map
- Net price and loan scenario board
- Scholarship and transfer-path planner
- Parent/student concern cards
- Tradeoff voting lane
- College-without-going-broke script
- Shareable family agreement memo

## Stitch prompt
Design a responsive family finance product called College Cost Truce Room for middle-income parents and college-bound students who need a shared plan before admissions, aid letters, and Parent PLUS loans become emotional fights. Users enter household budget limits, dream schools, in-state options, scholarship targets, transfer paths, parent debt boundaries, student contribution options, and non-negotiables; the app converts the tension into net-price scenarios, emotional tradeoff cards, a practical school-list mix, and a conversation script both sides can accept. Include a family budget boundary intake, dream-school attachment map, net price and loan scenario board, scholarship and transfer-path planner, parent/student concern cards, tradeoff voting lane, college-without-going-broke script, and shareable family agreement memo. Design direction: use the calm-consumer profile with reassuring spacing, warm but not beige-heavy surfaces, plain-language finance cards, gentle tension meters, and clear next-step controls so the experience feels like a family truce room instead of a debt calculator.

## Non-goals
- Do not implement a production backend in Phase A.
- Do not replace professional legal, medical, financial, or admissions advice.
- Do not turn the experience into a generic dashboard; preserve the core workflow.
