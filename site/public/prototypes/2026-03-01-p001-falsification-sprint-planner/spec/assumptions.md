# Assumptions (KNOWN / ASSUMPTION / UNKNOWN)

## KNOWN
- Early-stage teams often move from idea → build without explicit hypotheses.
- Writing down *kill criteria* reduces sunk-cost bias in decision-making.
- Simple prioritization heuristics (ICE/RICE variants) are widely understood.

## ASSUMPTION
- A lightweight single-page tool (no accounts) can still meaningfully improve experiment quality.
- Builders will accept “good enough” scoring if the UI is fast and prescriptive.
- Providing test-type templates (landing page, outreach, price test) reduces blank-page friction.

## UNKNOWN
- Will users actually use kill criteria when the experiment feels emotionally exciting?
- Which scoring inputs are most predictive (impact/confidence/effort vs. reach/time/cost)?
- Is the primary market solo builders or product teams (different language + workflows)?
- Do users want exports (markdown/Notion) or is “plan on screen” enough?
