# Assumptions (KNOWN / ASSUMPTION / UNKNOWN)

## KNOWN
- A thesis can be decomposed into: claim → mechanism → timeframe → metrics.
- Better questions tend to be specific, falsifiable, and tied to observable evidence.
- People routinely suffer from confirmation bias and underweight disconfirming evidence.

## ASSUMPTION
- Users will prefer a *question-first* tool over a “LLM summary” tool.
- The highest value comes from: (a) question quality and (b) tracking unanswered items, not from “answers.”
- A small set of reusable failure-mode categories can cover most theses (market, unit economics, competition, execution, regulation, narrative/positioning).

## UNKNOWN
- Which question taxonomy produces the highest perceived usefulness for investing theses vs product theses.
- Whether users will consistently do the follow-up work (collect evidence) or just read the questions.
- How to keep the tool from feeling like generic “devil’s advocate” output.
- Will users pay for this as a standalone tool, or only as a feature inside a broader research workflow.
