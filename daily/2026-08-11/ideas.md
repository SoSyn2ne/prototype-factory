## Final candidates

### p001 — Practical / Commercial — Meeting Replay Link Exposure Drill
- one-liner: Paste meeting replay/share links and get exposure status, blast radius, and a revoke sequence before private conversations leak.
- target user: startup ops, recruiters, sales leaders, and agencies using meeting-recording SaaS.
- key UX: paste up to five links → classify public/indexable/domain-only/private → map exposed participants/topics → drag revoke actions into order → export incident receipt.
- required UI sections: URL intake; exposure traffic lights; blast-radius map; revoke queue; owner assignments; proof receipt.
- assigned design profile: `operator-dense`.
- theme: 자산 방어 / reputation and confidential-information protection.
- source signals: S1, S4.
- reference_pattern: security link checker + incident runbook.
- twist: checks the external replay-link boundary and produces a revoke sequence, not a generic meeting dashboard.
- small_group_fit: any five-person team can paste its last meeting links and discover unsafe sharing in minutes.
- spread_trigger: shareable redacted “3 links revoked / 14 people protected” receipt.
- source_repo: not GitHub-derived; why_starred: not applicable.
- first_screen: five-link intake beside an exposure traffic-light strip and top revoke action.
- share_or_play_loop: export a redacted protection receipt to the incident channel.
- Stitch prompt:
```text
Design a responsive web app named “Meeting Replay Link Exposure Drill.” Build a dense first screen where a startup operator pastes up to five meeting replay URLs and immediately sees Public, Domain-only, Expired, or Private status. Include an exposure traffic-light strip, a participant/topic blast-radius map, a draggable revoke-order queue with owners, a before/after permission comparison, and a redacted incident receipt export. Use realistic sample links and show empty, scanning, risky, and resolved states. Do not imply live access; label checks as simulated. Design direction: follow the operator-dense profile with compact tables, crisp status chips, restrained navy/amber/red, square-ish cards, and high information density without a decorative hero.
```
