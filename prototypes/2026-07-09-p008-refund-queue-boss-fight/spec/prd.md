# PRD - Refund Queue Boss Fight

## One-liner
A playful travel-support simulator that turns airline refund escalation into a timed evidence-stacking boss fight.

## Problem
Travelers, consumer-rights creators, and portfolio viewers who understand refund support hell and want a funny but useful simulator. are reacting to fresh market/community signals but still solve the work with scattered screenshots, invoices, threads, or ad hoc spreadsheets. The prototype should turn that repeated pain into one focused first-screen workflow.

## Target user
Travelers, consumer-rights creators, and portfolio viewers who understand refund support hell and want a funny but useful simulator.

## Key UX
The player stacks screenshots, timelines, insurance notes, and card-issuer options against a “support boss” before the deadline bar empties.

## Required UI sections
- Cancelled trip case card
- Evidence cards: notice, receipt, chat, policy, insurance
- Boss meter for generic replies and delay tactics
- Escalation move selector
- Victory receipt with next real-world steps

## Design profile
- playful-experimental

## Theme
- Consumer rights / refund escalation as game

## Reference pattern
- timed card battler + consumer escalation checklist

## Twist
- Makes a boring refund checklist memorable by turning delay tactics into enemies and evidence into moves.

## Small group fit
- Travelers already post “what should I do?” threads; this gives them a fast, funny rehearsal surface.

## Spread trigger
- The victory receipt and boss screen are easy to screenshot and share with travel groups.

## Copy/paste Stitch prompt
Create a playful consumer-rights game prototype called “Refund Queue Boss Fight.” Show a cancelled-trip case card, evidence cards, a boss meter for generic support replies, deadline timer, escalation move selector, and victory receipt with next steps. Design direction: use the playful-experimental profile with energetic contrast, readable game UI, one clear workflow, and humorous labels that do not hide the practical escalation path.

## Non-goals
- Do not implement a custom local demo in Phase A.
- Do not present source signals as proof of market size.
- Do not turn the product into a generic dashboard without the specific trigger and workflow.

## Success metrics
- A viewer understands the target user and core action within 5 seconds.
- The primary workflow can be inferred from the first screen without explanatory marketing copy.
- The Stitch output preserves the assigned design profile and includes all required sections.
