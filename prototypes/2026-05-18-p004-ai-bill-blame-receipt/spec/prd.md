# PRD — AI Bill Blame Receipt

## Problem
- A playful utility-bill explainer that turns rising power costs and data-center arguments into a shareable neighborhood blame receipt.
- Evidence sources: Signal 7, Signal 8.

## Target user
- Homeowners, renters, local reporters, community organizers, and curious residents trying to explain why electric bills jumped and how much of the anger is really about data centers, fuel, grid upgrades, or rate decisions.

## Key UX
- Upload or type a utility bill, choose a state/utility, add recent rate-case headlines or local data-center news, and the product breaks the bill into a theatrical receipt: fuel, grid, data-center demand, taxes, weather, usage, and approved rate hikes, with uncertainty labels and a share card for neighborhood discussion.

## Required UI sections
- bill upload / manual entry stage
- rate-change timeline
- bill component blame stack
- data-center claim meter
- weather and usage normalizer
- public hearing / regulator note cards
- shareable neighborhood receipt poster

## Design profile
- playful-experimental

## Stitch prompt
Design a playful experimental web product called AI Bill Blame Receipt for homeowners, renters, local reporters, community organizers, and curious residents trying to explain why electric bills jumped. Users upload or type a utility bill, choose a state and utility, add recent rate-case headlines or local data-center news, and the app breaks the bill into a theatrical receipt: fuel, grid upgrades, data-center demand, taxes, weather, household usage, and approved rate hikes, with uncertainty labels and a share card for neighborhood discussion. Include a bill upload or manual entry stage, rate-change timeline, bill component blame stack, data-center claim meter, weather and usage normalizer, public hearing/regulator note cards, and shareable neighborhood receipt poster. Design direction: use a playful-experimental aesthetic with bold receipt-paper typography, electric yellow and civic blue accents, animated blame stamps, cheeky but factual labels, and a clear caveat system so it feels viral without pretending uncertain data is exact.
