# PRD — Heat Break Proof Kit

## Problem
- A field-ops compliance kit that turns heat-risk forecasts, crew schedules, and rest breaks into proof-ready safety logs before summer inspections.
- External signal references:
  - Signal 1: OSHA — Heat Injury and Illness Prevention rulemaking — https://www.osha.gov/heat-exposure/rulemaking
  - Signal 2: NFIB — small businesses oppose federal heat standard — https://www.nfib.com/news/press-release/small-businesses-oppose-overreaching-federal-heat-standard/

## Target user
- Small contractors, landscapers, survey crews, warehouse supervisors, delivery dispatchers, and field-service owners who must protect crews from heat risk without a full safety department.

## Key UX
- Enter jobsite location, shift times, crew roles, temperature/heat-index triggers, and supervisor notes; the app builds a heat plan, break schedule, acclimatization checklist, training receipts, and inspection-ready logbook.

## Required UI sections
- jobsite heat-risk intake
- crew acclimatization roster
- cool-down break scheduler
- water/shade/PPE checklist
- supervisor observation log
- OSHA/NFIB paperwork burden meter
- inspection-ready proof packet

## Stitch prompt
Design a tablet-first field-ops product called Heat Break Proof Kit for small contractors, landscapers, survey crews, warehouse supervisors, delivery dispatchers, and field-service owners who need heat-safety compliance without a full safety department. Users should enter jobsite location, shift times, crew roles, heat-index triggers, acclimatization status, water/shade/PPE availability, and supervisor notes, then generate a heat plan, cool-down break schedule, training receipt, observation log, and inspection-ready proof packet. Include a jobsite heat-risk intake, crew acclimatization roster, cool-down break scheduler, water/shade/PPE checklist, supervisor observation log, OSHA/NFIB paperwork burden meter, and inspection-ready proof packet. Design direction: use a field-ops aesthetic with large glove-friendly controls, sun/heat color bands, map-and-shift cards, offline-ready checklists, and rugged proof stamps so the UI feels built for a foreman on a hot jobsite rather than an HR portal.
