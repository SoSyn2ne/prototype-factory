The Summer I Learned to Build

It started with a pile of scrap wood behind the garage. I wasn't looking to build anything specific, just looking for a way to waste a Tuesday afternoon before junior year started.

Show, don't tell! What kind of wood? Plywood? 2x4s?

My grandfather used to say that every piece of material has a purpose, even if you don't know what it is yet. I picked up a warped two-by-four. It was heavy, splintered, and smelled like old rain. It felt like potential.

I dragged out the rusty circular saw. It was dangerous but I didn't care. I wanted to see if I could make something out of nothing. The first cut was crooked, the blade protesting against the damp grain.

Good sensory details here. Keep going with the sound of the saw.

Hours turned into days. The pile of wood slowly transformed into a rudimentary chair. It wobbled, one leg was distinctly shorter than the others, and sitting on it practically guaranteed a splinter. But when I finally sat on it, looking out over the driveway, it was the most comfortable seat in the world because I had made it.

That chair is still in the garage. It reminds me that progress isn't about perfection on the first try; it's about the willingness to pick up the tools and make the first, imperfect cut.

* Don't forget to mention the smell of sawdust!
