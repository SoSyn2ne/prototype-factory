# PRD - Korea Caregiver Debt Splitter

## Problem
- A family care-cost room that shows Korean siblings the monthly caregiver burn, debt risk, and fair contribution plan before resentment explodes.
- Evidence: S5 (Chosun, 1M Korean seniors care costs drive family debt, Feb 2026); S6 (A Place for Mom, 2026 home-care cost ranges).

## Target user
- Korean adult children coordinating parent care, siblings living apart, hospital discharge planners, and family caregivers.

## Key UX
- A family negotiation workspace that translates caregiver quotes, hospital nights, long-term-care grade, sibling income, available time, parent assets, and debt limits into a monthly rota and contribution agreement.

## Required UI sections
- Monthly care burn header in won with debt-risk bands
- Care schedule builder for hospital sitter, home caregiver, family shifts, and respite gaps
- Sibling capacity table for income, location, available nights, guilt limits, and cash ceiling
- Insurance and subsidy checklist for long-term-care grade, benefits, and uncovered costs
- Fair-split simulator comparing equal, income-weighted, time-weighted, and parent-asset drawdown models
- Conflict log for promises, missed shifts, emergency calls, and resentment triggers
- Family meeting packet with contribution plan, next review date, and care-quality watchlist

## Assigned design profile
- calm-consumer

## Theme
- 건강 / 자산 방어 / 외로움 - aging parent urgency, sibling fairness, caregiver debt, and emotional load.

## Practical vs fun label

## Stitch prompt
Build a calm consumer web app prototype called "Korea Caregiver Debt Splitter" for Korean adult children coordinating a parent's caregiver costs and sibling contributions. The first screen must be a working family care-cost room with a monthly care burn header, care schedule builder, sibling capacity table, insurance/subsidy checklist, fair-split simulator, conflict log, and family meeting packet. Use realistic sample data: 3.7M won monthly private caregiver quote, hospital discharge deadline, three siblings with different income and distance, long-term-care grade uncertainty, parent savings, night-shift gaps, and one sibling refusing equal payment. Design direction: use the calm-consumer profile with warm neutral surfaces, reassuring step flows, gentle warning accents, and emotionally steady language for a painful family money conversation. Do not make a marketing landing page; the first screen must be the working app.
