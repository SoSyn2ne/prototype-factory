# PRD — Windowlight Dinner Club

## Problem
- A dreamy social ritual prototype where solo eaters share anonymous dinner moods, ambient tablescapes, and tiny acts of company instead of eating alone with a dead scroll.
- Many lonely adults want company during meals, but chat-heavy social products feel performative, noisy, or too intimate for a small daily ritual.

## Target user
- Urban solo dwellers, remote workers, and night eaters who want a softer feeling of company during dinner without the pressure of video calls or constant chat.

## Constraints
- Time: The emotional hook should be obvious in under 2 minutes.
- Budget: Version one should simulate ambient social presence without real-time scaling complexity.
- Platform: Desktop-first concept demo focused on atmosphere and ritual design.

## Non-goals
- A full social network or messaging app.
- Dating or matching mechanics.
- Video chat or identity-heavy profiles.

## Success metrics
- Users instantly understand the ambient-company concept.
- The first screen feels emotionally warm and screenshot-worthy.
- The ritual feels less lonely without requiring active conversation.

## Notes
- Theme: 외로움.
- Design profile: playful-experimental.
- Core UI sections: ambient dinner stage, mood table selector, anonymous presence feed, tiny gesture tray, room warmth meter, collectible scene capture drawer.
- Stitch prompt: Design a whimsical desktop social ritual prototype called Windowlight Dinner Club for solo dwellers who want a gentler sense of company while eating alone. It should let users choose a dinner mood, enter a shared ambient room, exchange tiny anonymous gestures, and watch the table atmosphere shift as more people quietly join the ritual. Include an ambient dinner stage, a mood table selector, an anonymous presence feed, a tiny gesture tray, a room warmth meter, and a collectible scene capture drawer. Design direction: use a playful-experimental aesthetic with cinematic window-glow lighting, saturated dusk colors, soft glass overlays, and a memorable storybook composition so the product feels intimate, shareable, and emotionally warm without turning into a chat app.
