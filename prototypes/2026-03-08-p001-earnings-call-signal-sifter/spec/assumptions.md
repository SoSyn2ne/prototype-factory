# Assumptions (KNOWN / ASSUMPTION / UNKNOWN)

## KNOWN
- Transcripts are long, noisy, and contain repeated phrases; humans benefit from structured extraction.
- In semis, guidance / demand / supply / margin / capital allocation are recurring decision axes.
- Good decision-making improves when you write down “what would prove me wrong.”

## ASSUMPTION
- For many users, forcing **exactly 5** signals (one per category) reduces cognitive load vs. a free-form list.
- A confidence slider is a useful proxy for uncertainty even without calibrated probabilities.
- A dedicated “falsification queue” increases the chance the user follows up (rather than forgetting).

## UNKNOWN
- Whether users prefer per-signal prompts (category-specific questions) vs. a generic card format.
- Whether the 5-category taxonomy fits non-semi companies without feeling arbitrary.
- Whether users will paste full transcripts vs. only prepared remarks / highlights.
