# Falsification checklist (5–7)

1. Do users judge the generated questions as *generic* (“I’ve seen this before”) rather than thesis-specific?
2. When asked to answer the questions, do users fail to identify any new unknowns or disconfirming evidence (i.e., it doesn’t change behavior)?
3. Does evidence tracking add friction without improving outcomes (users abandon before marking items answered/unknown)?
4. Are the failure-mode categories wrong for the domain (e.g., investing users want very different buckets than product users)?
5. Do users interpret outputs as financial advice (risk/compliance issue) despite framing as “questions only”?
6. Can a simple template (“write 10 hard questions yourself”) outperform the tool for most users (no defensible advantage)?
7. Does the tool encourage over-analysis (more questions) instead of decision quality (fewer, sharper unknowns)?
