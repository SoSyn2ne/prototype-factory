# Falsification checklist (5–7)

1. Users refuse to enter constraints because it feels “too gossipy” or emotionally taxing, even if the UI is fast.
2. Hotspot ranking feels obvious (“yeah, of course my divorced parents are #1”) and doesn’t change decisions or timing.
3. Users only care about the final seating chart; a “friction map” without auto-assignment is not valuable enough to use.
4. Pairwise constraints are the wrong abstraction; most real constraints are group/table-level (“all college friends together”) and the tool misleads.
5. The 1–3 weight scale is either too coarse or too confusing; users can’t apply it consistently.
6. The matrix/visualization breaks down past ~25 guests and becomes unreadable, so users abandon the tool.
7. The tool surfaces “high friction” but provides no actionable next step, so it increases anxiety rather than reducing it.
