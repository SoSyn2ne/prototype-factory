# PRD — Creator Sponsorship Fulfillment Radar

## One-liner
A campaign ops console that flags sponsorship deliverables at risk before creator payouts, brand approvals, or posting windows slip.

## Problem
- Sponsorship revenue leaks when briefs, draft assets, approvals, and posting slots drift across email and chat.
- Agencies and brand teams struggle to see which campaigns are one missed step away from delayed revenue or angry partners.
- Operators need a clean command view that shows fulfillment risk and the fastest unblock action.

## Target user
- Primary: creator managers, influencer agencies, and in-house brand partnership operators.
- Secondary: finance or account leads watching payout timing and campaign completion health.

## Why now
- Creator businesses run more concurrent micro-campaigns with tighter posting windows.
- Brand approvals and payout terms are increasingly tied to on-time fulfillment.
- Teams have asset status scattered across tools without a unifying risk view.

## Key UX
- Scan campaigns by fulfillment risk and deadline pressure.
- Open one partnership and review deliverable status, approval sequence, and missing dependencies in a single screen.
- Trigger the next unblock move and understand payout exposure immediately.

## Required UI sections
- Campaign health hero
- Sponsorship risk board
- Deliverable status matrix
- Approval timeline
- Unblock action panel
- Payout exposure rail

## Constraints
- Time: Phase A only; no custom demo implementation.
- Budget: should work for lean agency and brand ops teams.
- Platform: desktop web operations dashboard.

## Non-goals
- Full contract management.
- Direct social publishing.
- Automated creator payment execution.

## Success metrics
- Reduce late sponsorship deliverables.
- Improve on-time campaign completion.
- Lower payout and approval delays caused by missing operational visibility.

## Stitch-ready design direction
Use a polished creator-business aesthetic with charcoal, warm white, and saturated violet accents; emphasize clean hierarchy, editorial card layouts, and a product feel that is premium, deadline-aware, and commercially sharp rather than playful.
