# PRD — Layoff Comeback Offer Sprint

## Problem
- A career rebound command center that helps recently laid-off professionals rebuild their story, target faster-paying roles, and sequence weekly comeback moves before savings run thin.
- Generic job-search tools do not connect shrinking savings, confidence loss, and narrative repair into one operational recovery surface.

## Target user
- Recently laid-off mid-career workers who need an emotionally tolerable but brutally practical plan to secure their next offer within 30 to 60 days.

## Constraints
- Time: Users should identify their top comeback moves in under 10 minutes.
- Budget: Version one should avoid resume parsing dependencies and work with manual entry.
- Platform: Desktop-first workflow for concentrated weekly planning and review.

## Non-goals
- A mass job board or recruiter marketplace.
- Automated application spam or ghostwriting everything for the user.
- Long-term learning management or full career coaching replacement.

## Success metrics
- Users can identify the highest-probability next moves in one screen.
- Users understand how many weeks of runway remain and what that implies for role selection.
- The workflow makes weekly progress feel measurable instead of emotionally foggy.

## Notes
- Theme: 역전.
- Design profile: operator-dense.
- Core UI sections: runway and urgency strip, target role pipeline board, story gap fixer panel, weekly comeback sprint planner, interview pressure tracker, offer recovery plan drawer.
- Stitch prompt: Design a desktop job-recovery command center called Layoff Comeback Offer Sprint for recently laid-off professionals who need to restore income fast. It should help them see their financial runway, choose which target roles are most realistic, repair weak story gaps, and execute a weekly comeback sprint toward the next offer before confidence collapses. Include a runway and urgency strip, a target role pipeline board, a story gap fixer panel, a weekly comeback sprint planner, an interview pressure tracker, and an offer recovery plan drawer. Design direction: use an operator-dense aesthetic with dark slate structure, crisp white and fog surfaces, one focused electric blue action accent, and highly scannable momentum cues so the product feels urgent, disciplined, and comeback-oriented rather than motivational fluff.
