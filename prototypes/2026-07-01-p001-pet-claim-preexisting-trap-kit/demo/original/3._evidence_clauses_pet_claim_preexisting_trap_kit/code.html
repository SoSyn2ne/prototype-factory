Preexisting-Clause Explainer

Insurance companies rely on specific legal definitions to deny claims. We break down their jargon into plain language so you know exactly what to argue.

gavel The Insurance Jargon

"Claim denied due to pre-existing condition based on observation of clinical signs prior to the policy effective date."

translate What it actually means

What is a clinical sign? It is any physical abnormality or symptom observed by a vet, even if no diagnosis was made at the time.

The Catch: They are arguing that a vague symptom (like a mild limp) noted 6 months ago is directly related to the current major surgery, making it "pre-existing."

Defense Strategy:

We need to prove the past symptom was an isolated, cured incident, unrelated to the current diagnosis. This requires a strong letter from your treating vet explicitly stating the two events are unconnected.

gavel The Insurance Jargon

"Condition is excluded under the bilateral condition clause."

translate What it actually means

Bilateral condition: A condition that can happen on both sides of the body (e.g., ACL tears, hip dysplasia, eye issues).

The Catch: If your pet had a problem with their left knee before the policy started, the insurance company often automatically excludes the right knee, even if it happens years later, claiming they are related.
