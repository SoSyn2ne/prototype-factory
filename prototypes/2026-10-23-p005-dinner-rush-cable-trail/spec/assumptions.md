# Assumptions — Dinner Rush Cable Trail

## KNOWN

- The final Phase A title and one-liner are fixed in daily/2026-10-23/ideas.md.
- The target user is: independent restaurants, general managers, shift leads, POS/KDS installers, and support desks.
- The observed workaround is: handwritten cable labels, photos in staff chat, vendor phone trees, and “restart everything”.
- Exactly one PF design profile is assigned: field-ops.
- Source references are: S06.
- Phase A intentionally contains no custom demo.

## ASSUMPTION

- The target user will understand the mechanic without a guided onboarding tour: scan the dead endpoint, walk a highlighted physical cable trail, test one edge at a time, then activate a role-specific manual service mode.
- The concrete output will be worth saving or sharing: printable label sheet, live cable trail, manual-service role card, and support-ready action log.
- The selected profile makes the high-pressure moment easier to read, not merely prettier.
- The arrival path can recruit an initial test group: restaurant-owner communities, POS/KDS installers, managed-service partners, search phrase “POS down dinner rush”.
- The proposed spread trigger is socially acceptable: a vendor-ready trail card names the failed edge and every action already tried; staff can also share the paper-service route.

## UNKNOWN

- Real willingness to pay and the final economic buyer.
- Whether the source pain repeats often enough outside the observed community.
- Which integrations or hardware constraints would dominate production.
- Whether the first validation test produces a meaningful behavior change.
